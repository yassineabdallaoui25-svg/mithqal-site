Employer cost and contributions

Enter a gross salary: see what the employer really pays, what goes to the CNSS on both sides, and what reaches the employee’s account.

How the cost is calculated

  1. Social benefits: 8.98% of gross pay, capped at 6,000 MAD a month (538.80 MAD at most).
  2. Family allowances: 6.40% of gross pay, no cap, paid by the employer only.
  3. AMO: 4.11% of gross pay, no cap (2.26% basic AMO and 1.85% solidarity share).
  4. Vocational training tax: 1.60% of gross pay, no cap.
  5. Total cost = gross + employer contributions. The employee pays 4.48% CNSS (capped), 2.26% AMO and income tax on the gross.

FAQ

Are employer contributions always 21.09%?

No. 21.09% applies up to 6,000 MAD gross. Above that, the capped part (8.98%) stops growing: at 30,000.00 MAD gross, contributions are only 13.9% of the salary.

Why does the employee get only 76.50 MAD out of every 100?

At 8,000 MAD gross, the employer pays 9,507.60 MAD in total. The employee receives 7,273.65 MAD net: the rest goes to contributions (both sides) and income tax.

Are CIMR and top-up health cover included?

No. Supplementary pension (CIMR) and top-up health insurance depend on each company. They come on top of the cost shown here.

Do some companies pay less AMO?

Companies that already had health cover before AMO became general may fall under a special arrangement. Check your situation with the CNSS.
